Grammar and Sentence Skills

Build accurate sentences by controlling agreement, punctuation, and sentence structure.

Key idea

Clear grammar helps readers understand who is acting, what is happening, and how ideas are connected.

Subject and Verb Agreement

The subject and verb must agree in number. A singular subject takes a singular verb, while a plural subject takes a plural verb.

The girl walks. The girls walk.

Sentence Parts

The subject tells who or what the sentence is about. The predicate tells what the subject does or is.

Subject: The careful driver

Predicate: stopped at the crossing.

Sentence Types

A statement gives information. A question asks something. A command gives an instruction. An exclamation shows strong feeling.

We are ready. Are we ready? Be ready! What a wonderful day!

Punctuation

Use a full stop for a statement, a question mark for a question, and an exclamation mark for strong feeling. Commas help separate items and introductory phrases.

Worked Example

Choose the correct verb: The group of players (is / are) ready.

The correct answer is “is”. The main subject is “group”, which is singular.

Remember

Find the main subject, match the verb to it, and reread the full sentence after adding punctuation.
