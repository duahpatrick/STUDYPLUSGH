Writing and Composition

Plan, draft, revise and edit extended pieces of writing with clear purpose and audience.

Key idea

Strong writing develops through careful planning, drafting, feedback and revision.

Preparing to Write

Identify your purpose (inform, persuade, entertain), audience (who reads this?) and format (letter, essay, story). Plan your ideas using lists, mind maps or outlines. Research if necessary and gather examples or evidence.

Paragraph Structure

A paragraph develops one main idea. Topic sentence states the main point. Supporting sentences explain, illustrate or prove it. Concluding sentence summarises or links to the next paragraph.

Topic: "Exercise improves health." Support: "Regular movement strengthens the heart." Conclusion: "These benefits make exercise essential."

Different Types of Writing

Narrative tells events in order. Descriptive creates pictures with words. Expository explains an idea or process. Persuasive argues for an opinion using evidence and logic.

Each type has different organisation and techniques.

Drafting and Revision

First draft: get ideas down without worrying about perfection. Revision: re-read, add missing information, remove unnecessary words, improve clarity. Focus on content first, mechanics later.

Editing and Proofreading

Editing: check grammar, punctuation, spelling. Proofread: read slowly to catch errors. Use a checklist: are sentences complete? Do subjects and verbs agree? Is punctuation correct?

Quick Check

What is the difference between revising and editing?

Revising improves ideas, organisation and clarity. Editing fixes grammar, punctuation and spelling.
