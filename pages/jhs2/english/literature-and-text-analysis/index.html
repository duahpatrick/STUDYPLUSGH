Literature and Text Analysis

Analyse literature by examining plot, character, theme and literary techniques.

Key idea

Understanding how writers construct meaning helps us appreciate literature and analyse what we read.

Story Elements: Plot and Setting

Plot: the sequence of events. Exposition introduces characters and setting. Rising action builds suspense. Climax is the turning point. Falling action leads to resolution.

Setting: where and when the story takes place. Setting can affect mood and influence character decisions.

Characters and Characterisation

Characterisation is how writers reveal what characters are like. Direct: the author tells us. Indirect: we learn through actions, dialogue, thoughts and what others say.

Direct: "Ama was brave." Indirect: "Ama stepped forward when everyone else stepped back."

Theme and Mood

Theme is the main idea or message of a story: friendship, courage, loss. A story might have multiple themes. Mood is the feeling created: happy, sad, tense, peaceful. Writers create mood through setting, colour words and events.

Literary Techniques

Metaphor compares things: "Time is a river." Simile uses "like" or "as": "Quiet as a mouse." Personification gives human qualities to objects: "The wind whispered." Imagery uses sensory words to create pictures.

Point of View and Perspective

First-person: narrator is a character ("I"). Third-person limited: narrator knows one character's thoughts. Third-person omniscient: narrator knows all characters' thoughts. Point of view affects what readers know and how we connect with characters.

Quick Check

What is the difference between plot and theme?

Plot is what happens (the events). Theme is the deeper meaning or message the author wants readers to understand.
