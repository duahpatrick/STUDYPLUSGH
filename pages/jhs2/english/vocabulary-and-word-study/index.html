Vocabulary and Word Study

Use context, word parts, and relationships to understand and use new words.

Key idea

A word's meaning becomes clearer when you study its context, form, related words, and role in the sentence.

Context Clues

Nearby words can define, describe, compare, or give an example of an unfamiliar word.

The path was narrow, unlike the wide road beside it. The contrast shows that narrow means not wide.

Synonyms and Antonyms

Synonyms have similar meanings, while antonyms have opposite meanings.

Synonym: rapid and quick

Antonym: ancient and modern

Prefixes and Suffixes

A prefix comes before a root word and can change its meaning. A suffix comes after a root word and can change its form or meaning.

un + happy = unhappy; teach + er = teacher.

Using New Words

Check the part of speech, pronunciation, spelling, and meaning. Then use the word in your own sentence so it becomes easier to remember.

Worked Example

In “The generous woman shared her food with everyone,” what does generous mean?

Generous means willing to give or share more than is expected. The action of sharing food provides the clue.

Remember

Do not choose a meaning from memory alone. Read the whole sentence, look for clues, and test the meaning in context.

Test Your Understanding

Practise context clues, synonyms, antonyms, prefixes, and suffixes.
