Reading and Comprehension

Read actively, identify important ideas, and answer questions with evidence.

Key idea

Strong comprehension comes from connecting the writer's words, the structure of the passage, and sensible inferences.

Read with a Purpose

Before reading, look at the title and predict the subject. While reading, notice repeated ideas, important details, and words that show contrast or cause.

However signals contrast; therefore signals a result; because signals a reason.

Main Ideas and Details

The main idea is the central message. Supporting details explain, illustrate, or prove it. A good summary includes the main idea and the most important supporting points.

Literal and Inferred Meaning

A literal answer is stated directly. An inference is a reasonable conclusion based on clues in the passage and what you already know.

Clue: The road was wet and people carried umbrellas.

Inference: It had probably been raining.

Vocabulary in Context

Use nearby words, examples, contrasts, and the overall sentence to work out an unfamiliar word. Replace the word with a possible meaning and check whether the sentence still makes sense.

Worked Example

If a passage says Ama packed a torch before entering the dark room, what can you infer?

You can infer that Ama expected the room to have little or no light.

Remember

Read the question twice, return to the passage, support answers with details, and avoid adding information that the writer did not suggest.

Guided Passage 1: The School Garden

When the rainy season began, the students of Adom JHS started a garden behind their classroom block. Their science teacher, Madam Esi, divided the land into small plots and assigned each group a responsibility. One group loosened the soil, another planted seeds, and a third group collected dry leaves for compost.

At first, some students thought the work was too difficult. The sun was hot, and the young plants needed water every morning. However, the groups created a simple timetable. Students who lived nearby watered the garden before school, while others checked the plants during break time. After several weeks, the garden became green and healthy.

The students used some vegetables for a cooking lesson and sold the rest to teachers. They saved the money to buy more seeds. The garden taught them that patience, cooperation, and careful planning can turn a small idea into something useful for the whole school.

Question: What is the main lesson of the passage?

Solved answer: Patience, cooperation, and careful planning can turn a small idea into something useful for the community. The final paragraph gives this message directly.

Question: Why did the students create a timetable?

Solved answer: They created it so the garden would receive regular care even though the students had different schedules.

Guided Passage 2: A Better Way to Travel

Kojo usually walked to the community library after school. One afternoon, he noticed that a narrow footbridge over a stream had become unsafe. Several wooden boards were loose, and the handrail had broken.

Kojo did not cross the bridge. Instead, he returned to the community centre and reported the problem to the assembly member. He also helped write a notice asking residents to use the longer road until the bridge was repaired.

A week later, workers replaced the boards and fixed the handrail. The assembly member thanked Kojo for reporting the danger early. Kojo learned that being observant and speaking up can protect many people, even when the person who notices the problem is young.

Question: What can we infer about Kojo?

Solved answer: Kojo is observant, responsible, and concerned about other people’s safety. His actions, rather than a direct description, provide the evidence.

Question: Suggest a suitable title.

Solved answer: “Kojo Speaks Up” is suitable because the passage focuses on his decision to report the dangerous bridge.
