Oral English and Speech

Speak clearly, listen actively and communicate effectively in different situations.

Key idea

Clear speech, active listening and confident communication build strong relationships and help ideas spread.

Speaking Clearly and Confidently

Speak at a steady pace: not too fast (listeners can't keep up) or too slow (they lose interest). Use appropriate volume: loud enough to hear, not so loud it's uncomfortable. Pronounce words clearly. Make eye contact with listeners.

Pause to let ideas settle. Avoid filler words like "um" and "uh". Stand straight and use natural gestures.

Different Contexts

Formal speech (presentations, interviews): use Standard English, plan carefully, maintain professional tone. Informal speech (conversations, storytelling): more relaxed, use appropriate register for your audience.

Formal: "Good morning. Thank you for listening." Informal: "Hi everyone, so here's what happened..."

Presentations and Public Speaking

Structure: introduction (introduce topic and yourself), body (main points with examples), conclusion (summarise and invite questions). Use notes, not a script. Practice beforehand. Use visual aids to support, not distract.

Active Listening

Listen to understand, not just to reply. Maintain eye contact. Don't interrupt. Ask clarifying questions. Show interest through body language. Remember key points and reflect back what you heard.

Discussion and Debate

In discussions, build on others' ideas and explain your thinking. In debates, present strong arguments, use evidence, listen to opposing views respectfully. Stay calm and focused on ideas, not personal attacks.

Quick Check

Why is active listening important in a discussion?

Active listening helps you understand others' ideas, build better responses, and show respect for different views.

Test Your Understanding

Practice speaking clearly, listening actively and presenting ideas.
