Algebraic Expressions

Use letters, terms, and operations to describe unknown values and number patterns.

Key idea

An algebraic expression represents a quantity using numbers, variables, and operations without an equals sign.

Terms and Coefficients

Terms are separated by plus or minus signs. A coefficient is the number multiplying a variable.

In 5x + 3y - 7, the terms are 5x, 3y, and -7.

Like and Unlike Terms

Like terms have the same variable parts and can be combined. Unlike terms have different variable parts and cannot be combined.

4a + 3a = 7a

4a + 3b cannot be simplified further.

Substitution

Replace a variable with its value, then follow the order of operations.

If x = 4, then 3x + 2 = 3(4) + 2 = 14.

Expanding Brackets

Multiply the term outside a bracket by every term inside the bracket.

2(x + 5) = 2x + 10

Worked Example

Simplify 6p + 2 - 3p + 5.

Collect like terms: 6p - 3p = 3p and 2 + 5 = 7. Answer: 3p + 7.

Remember

Keep the sign attached to each term, combine only like terms, and check your substitution carefully.
