Data and Probability

Collect, organise, describe, and interpret information with confidence.

Key idea

Good data displays make information easier to compare, while probability describes how likely events are to happen.

Collecting Data

Data can be collected by asking questions, observing, measuring, or counting. A tally table helps record repeated results quickly.

Favourite fruit: mango 8, orange 5, banana 7. The total number surveyed is 20.

Tables and Charts

Bar charts compare categories, line graphs show change over time, and pie charts show parts of a whole. Always check the title, labels, and scale.

Mean, Median, and Mode

The mean is the total divided by the number of values. The median is the middle value after ordering. The mode is the most frequent value.

For 2, 3, 3, 5, 7: mean = 4, median = 3, mode = 3.

Probability

Probability measures how likely an event is. It ranges from 0, impossible, to 1, certain.

Probability = favourable outcomes ÷ total possible outcomes.

Worked Example

A bag has 3 red counters and 2 blue counters. What is the probability of choosing a red counter?

There are 3 favourable red counters and 5 counters altogether. Probability = 3/5.

Remember

Order data before finding the median, use the correct chart scale, and make sure the denominator counts all possible outcomes.
