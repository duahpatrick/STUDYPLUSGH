Angles and Shapes

Use angle facts and shape properties to reason through geometry problems.

Key idea

Angles and shapes follow reliable properties that help us calculate unknown measures and explain relationships.

Types of Angles

An acute angle is less than 90°, a right angle is exactly 90°, an obtuse angle is between 90° and 180°, and a straight angle is 180°.

45° is acute; 90° is right; 120° is obtuse.

Angle Relationships

Angles on a straight line add to 180°. Angles around a point add to 360°. Vertically opposite angles are equal.

x + 70° = 180°, so x = 110°

Angles around a point: 90° + 80° + x = 360°, so x = 190°

Triangles and Quadrilaterals

The interior angles of a triangle add to 180°. The interior angles of a quadrilateral add to 360°.

A triangle with angles 55° and 65° has a third angle of 60°.

Shape Properties

Triangles have three sides. Quadrilaterals have four sides. A rectangle has four right angles, while a square has four equal sides and four right angles.

Worked Example

Two angles in a triangle are 48° and 72°. Find the third angle.

Angles in a triangle total 180°. Third angle = 180° - 48° - 72° = 60°.

Remember

Identify the shape or relationship first, write the total angle you need, then subtract known angles carefully.
