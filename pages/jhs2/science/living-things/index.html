Living Things

Study cells, organisms, classification and how living things adapt to their environments.

Key idea

All living things are made of cells and share common characteristics including growth, reproduction and response to environment.

Cells: The Basic Unit of Life

All living things are made of cells. Plant cells contain a cell wall, chloroplasts and large vacuole. Animal cells lack these. The nucleus controls cell activities. The mitochondria release energy.

Characteristics of Living Things

Living things: grow and develop, reproduce (make copies), respond to environment, obtain and use energy, maintain stable internal conditions (homeostasis), and pass traits to offspring.

Classification of Organisms

Kingdom: largest group (animals, plants, fungi, protists, bacteria). Phylum, class, order, family, genus, species: increasingly specific groupings. Each organism has a unique scientific name (genus and species).

Humans: Kingdom Animalia, Phylum Chordata, Class Mammalia, Order Primates, Family Hominidae, Genus Homo, Species sapiens.

Adaptation and Evolution

Adaptation: trait that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment. Structural adaptations: physical features (thick fur for cold). Behavioural adaptations: actions (migration to find food). Adaptations develop over many generations.

Life Cycles

Many organisms go through different stages: birth, growth, reproduction, death. Some undergo metamorphosis (complete change in form): egg → larva → pupa → adult.

Quick Check

What is the difference between a structural adaptation and a behavioural adaptation?

Structural is a physical feature (like thick fur). Behavioural is an action or habit (like migration).
