Environment and Ecosystems

Explore relationships between organisms and their environment, and understand human impact.

Key idea

Ecosystems are communities of organisms interacting with each other and their physical environment.

Ecosystems and Food Chains

An ecosystem includes living things and their physical environment. Producers (plants) make food from sunlight. Consumers eat plants or other consumers. Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead material.

A food chain shows this flow: grass → grasshopper → bird → hawk.

Biodiversity

Biodiversity is the variety of different species in an area. Greater biodiversity makes ecosystems more stable and resilient. Different species fill different roles: some control pests, some pollinate crops, some purify water.

Pollution and Its Effects

Air pollution: from factories, vehicles, burning fuels. Water pollution: from industries, agriculture, plastic waste. Soil pollution: from chemicals and waste. Pollution harms organisms, reduces biodiversity and can cause diseases.

Endangered Species and Extinction

Species become endangered when their population drops dangerously low. Causes: habitat loss, pollution, overhunting, climate change. Extinction is permanent loss. Conservation protects species through protected areas, breeding programs and law enforcement.

Conservation and Sustainability

Conservation means protecting ecosystems and species. Sustainable practices meet current needs without harming future generations. Examples: renewable energy, sustainable fishing, protected forests, reforestation.

Quick Check

What is the role of decomposers in an ecosystem?

Decomposers break down dead plants and animals, returning nutrients to the soil for producers to use.
