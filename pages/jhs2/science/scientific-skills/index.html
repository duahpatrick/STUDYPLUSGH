JHS 2 Science

Scientific Skills

Apply observation, investigation and data analysis to explore scientific questions.

Key idea

Scientists use systematic methods to investigate questions and support conclusions with evidence.

The Scientific Method

Observation: describe what you see. Question: ask why or how. Hypothesis: make a testable prediction. Experiment: test the hypothesis. Analysis: examine results. Conclusion: answer your question with evidence.

Designing Fair Tests

Independent variable: what you change. Dependent variable: what you measure. Control variables: what you keep the same. A fair test changes only one variable at a time so you can see its effect clearly.

Testing plant growth: change light (independent), measure plant height (dependent), keep water and soil the same (controls).

Measurement and Units

Length: metres, centimetres. Mass: kilograms, grams. Volume: litres, millilitres. Temperature: degrees Celsius. Time: seconds, minutes. Use appropriate precision for your measurement.

Recording and Presenting Data

Record data accurately in tables. Use charts and graphs to show patterns: line graphs for changes over time, bar graphs for comparisons, pie charts for proportions. Proper labels and units are essential.

Safety in the Laboratory

Wear safety goggles and appropriate clothing. Follow instructions carefully. Know where safety equipment is. Never taste or smell unknown substances. Report spills and accidents immediately. Clean up properly after experiments.

Quick Check

In an experiment, what is the independent variable?

The independent variable is what you change or control. It is the factor you test to see how it affects the outcome.
