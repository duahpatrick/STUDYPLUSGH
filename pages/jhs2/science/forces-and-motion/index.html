Forces and Motion

Understand how forces cause movement and how motion can be described and predicted.

Key idea

Forces push or pull; they can start motion, stop motion or change direction.

What Is Force?

A force is a push or pull. Forces can start movement, stop movement or change direction. Forces are measured in Newtons (N). Gravity pulls objects toward Earth. Friction opposes motion.

Newton's Laws of Motion

First law: Objects in motion stay in motion, objects at rest stay at rest (unless a force acts). Second law: Acceleration depends on force and mass (F = ma). Third law: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Seatbelts work because your body wants to keep moving forward (first law). A rocket propels upward by pushing gas downward (third law).

Friction and Resistance

Friction is the force between surfaces that opposes motion. Static friction keeps objects from moving. Sliding friction occurs when objects slide. Air resistance opposes objects moving through air. Friction can be reduced by lubricating surfaces or reducing contact.

Simple Machines

Simple machines reduce the force needed to do work: lever (crowbar), pulley (rope over wheel), inclined plane (ramp), wedge (axe), screw (threaded bolt), wheel and axle (door handle).

Speed, Velocity and Acceleration

Speed is how fast something moves. Velocity is speed with direction. Acceleration is change in velocity (speeding up, slowing down or changing direction). Constant velocity means no acceleration.

Quick Check

What is the difference between speed and velocity?

Speed is how fast something moves. Velocity is speed with direction (e.g., 60 km/h north).
