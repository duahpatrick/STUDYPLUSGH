JHS 1 Mathematics

Introduction to Statistics

Collect, organize, represent and interpret data from everyday life.

Key idea

Statistics helps us make sense of information by organizing and comparing data carefully.

Data and Variables

Data are facts or information collected for a purpose. Qualitative data describe qualities, such as eye colour. Quantitative data use numbers, such as the number of books.

A population is the whole group being studied; a sample is a smaller group selected from it.

Tables and Tally Charts

A tally chart records how often values occur. Group tallies in fives to count quickly. A frequency is the number of times a value occurs.

Example: 2, 3, 2, 4, 3, 2

Frequencies: 2 occurs 3 times, 3 occurs 2 times and 4 occurs 1 time.

Mean, Median, Mode and Range

Mean = total of values / number of values. Median is the middle value after arranging data in order. Mode is the value that occurs most often. Range = highest value - lowest value.

For an even number of values, the median is the mean of the two middle values.

Charts and Graphs

Bar charts compare separate categories using bars with equal widths. A pictograph uses symbols, and its key tells what each symbol represents. A line graph shows how a quantity changes, often over time.

Label both axes and give the graph a title.

Worked Example

Find the mean and range of 4, 6, 7, 8 and 10.

Total = 35, so mean = 35 / 5 = 7. Range = 10 - 4 = 6.

Quick Check

Find the mode of 3, 5, 3, 6, 4, 3.
