JHS 1 English

English Vocabulary

Understand and use new words accurately in everyday communication.

Key idea

Learning new words helps us understand texts and express ideas with more confidence.

Meaning from Context

Context is the words and sentences around an unfamiliar word. Use nearby clues, examples and contrasts to work out meaning before checking a dictionary.


In “The path was narrow, so only one person could walk on it,” narrow means not wide.



Synonyms and Antonyms

Synonyms are words with similar meanings: big and large. Antonyms have opposite meanings: early and late. The best synonym depends on the sentence and level of formality.



Prefixes and Suffixes

A prefix comes before a root word and changes its meaning: un + kind = unkind. A suffix comes after a root word and may change meaning or word class: care + ful = careful; teach + er = teacher.



Common Word Families

Words in a family share a root: act, action, active, actively. Learning families helps with spelling and choosing the correct form in a sentence.



Interactive Check

What does “ancient” mean in “The museum displays ancient tools”?

Ancient means very old or from a long time ago.



Remember

Use context, compare word relationships, study word parts, and practise using new words in your own sentences.
