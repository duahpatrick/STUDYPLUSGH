Reading Comprehension

Read carefully, understand ideas and answer questions with evidence.

Key idea

Good readers look for the main idea, key details, and evidence before answering questions.

Before You Read

Look at the title and predict the topic. Read the passage once for the general meaning, then read it again to find details. Do not rush to the questions before understanding the passage.



Finding the Main Idea

The main idea is the most important point in a paragraph or passage. Supporting details explain, prove or give examples of it. Ask: “What is this passage mostly about?”


A title often gives a clue, but the main idea should be expressed as a complete thought.



Literal and Inferential Questions

A literal answer is stated directly in the passage. An inferential answer is worked out from clues. For inference, combine what the writer says with what you already know.

Vocabulary-in-context questions ask what a word means in that particular sentence. Read the words around it before choosing a meaning.



How to Answer Well

Read every question carefully, answer in a complete sentence when required, and use your own words without changing the meaning. Check the passage for evidence and make sure your answer matches the question word: who, what, when, where, why or how.



Interactive Check

Passage: “Ama carried an umbrella because dark clouds covered the sky.” Why did Ama carry an umbrella?

She carried it because the dark clouds suggested that it might rain.



Remember

Predict, read, reread, locate evidence, and answer exactly what is asked. A good reader explains answers using clues from the passage.
