Parts of Speech

Learn how words are grouped according to the job they do in a sentence.

Key idea

Every word in a sentence has a job. Once you know that job, grammar becomes much easier to understand and use.

What Are Parts of Speech?

Parts of speech are groups of words classified by the work they do in a sentence. A word's part of speech can change when its use changes.

Example: Water is a noun in “The water is cold,” but a verb in “Please water the plant.”

Nouns and Pronouns

A noun names a person, place, animal, thing or idea: teacher, Accra, goat, book, honesty. A proper noun names a particular person or place and begins with a capital letter.

A pronoun takes the place of a noun: I, you, he, she, it, we, they, them.

Akosua carried her books.

Akosua is a proper noun; her is a pronoun.

Verbs

A verb shows an action or a state: run, write, is, believe, think.

The boy runs fast.

Adjectives and Adverbs

An adjective describes a noun or pronoun: a bright room. An adverb describes a verb, adjective or another adverb and often tells how, when or where: She sang beautifully.

Prepositions, Conjunctions and Interjections

A preposition shows a relationship: in, on, under, beside, between. A conjunction joins words or ideas: and, but, because, or. An interjection expresses sudden feeling: Oh! Wow! Hurray!

Example: Wow! Kojo ran to school because he was late.

Interactive Check

Identify the adjective: “The hardworking farmer grows maize.”

Correct answer: hardworking. It describes the farmer.

Remember

Ask what job each word performs. Nouns name, pronouns replace nouns, verbs show action or state, adjectives describe nouns, adverbs add detail, prepositions show relationships, conjunctions join, and interjections express feeling.
