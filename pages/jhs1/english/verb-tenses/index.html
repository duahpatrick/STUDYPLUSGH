Verb Tenses

Show when an action happens by choosing the correct verb form.

Key idea

Tense tells us when an action happens, so choosing the correct verb form is essential for clear communication.

What Is Tense?

Tense shows the time of an action or state. The three basic tenses are present, past and future.

Present: I walk to school. Past: I walked to school yesterday. Future: I will walk to school tomorrow.



Present Tense

Use the simple present for habits, facts and repeated actions. Use -s or -es with he, she and it: “She reads.” Do not add -s after I, you, we or they: “They read.”


Present continuous uses am, is or are plus an -ing verb for an action happening now: “The pupils are studying.”



Past Tense

Use the simple past for a completed action. Regular verbs often take -ed: play/played, wash/washed. Some verbs are irregular: go/went, eat/ate, see/saw, come/came.


Past continuous uses was or were plus -ing: “We were walking when it rained.”



Future Tense

Use will plus the base verb for an action that will happen later: “I will visit Kumasi.” You can also use be going to for a plan: “They are going to practise.”



Interactive Check

Complete: “Yesterday, Kojo __ his homework.”

A. does   B. did   C. will do

B. did. “Yesterday” tells us the action happened in the past.



Remember

Look for time clues such as every day, now, yesterday and tomorrow. Make the verb agree with the subject and check irregular past forms.
