Matter

Understand materials and how they change.

Key idea

Matter is everything around us, and understanding its states and changes helps explain everyday life.

What Is Matter?

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. It is made of tiny particles that are always moving.


States of matter

Solids have fixed shape and volume. Liquids have fixed volume but take the shape of their container. Gases have neither fixed shape nor fixed volume.



Changes of State

Melting changes solid to liquid; freezing changes liquid to solid; evaporation changes liquid to gas; condensation changes gas to liquid. These are physical changes because no new substance is formed.


Example: ice gains heat and melts into water. Water loses heat and freezes into ice.



Physical and Chemical Change

A physical change affects form or state and is often reversible. A chemical change forms a new substance, such as rusting iron or burning paper.


Worked example

Dissolving salt in water is physical: the salt can be recovered by evaporation. Burning salt would be a chemical change.



Remember

Heating usually increases particle movement; cooling usually decreases it.
