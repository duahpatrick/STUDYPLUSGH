JHS 1 Science

Living Things

Discover how organisms are grouped and how they stay alive.

Key idea

Living things share common characteristics, and classifying them helps us understand life on Earth.

Characteristics of Life

Living things carry out nutrition, respiration, movement, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion and death. The acronym MRS GREN helps us remember these processes.


A bean seed may look inactive, but it respires and grows when it receives water, air and suitable warmth.



Cells and Organisation

The cell is the basic unit of life. Plant and animal cells have a cell membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus. Plant cells also have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole.


Worked example

Leaves contain chloroplasts, so they can absorb light for photosynthesis. Red blood cells are specialised to carry oxygen.



Classification

Classification groups organisms by shared features. Vertebrates have backbones; fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals are vertebrate groups. Invertebrates do not have backbones.


Classification helps us identify organisms and study their relationships.



Remember

All living things are made of cells and perform life processes.
